PART 3 · Model benchmarks

How to read the scores,
and who is good at what

Every new model launch comes with a "we're number one again" scorecard. This page first explains why those scores can't be taken at face value, then puts the models side by side in one table, and finally sums up what each model is good for.

01 · A word of caution

Why you can't fully trust these scores

Scores are useful, but they're "softer" than they look. Once you understand the five reasons below, you'll know how much to discount a "number one" headline.

1 · Grading their own exam

Most scores are reported by the vendors

On the same exam, the vendor decides how much thinking time to allow, which agent harness to use and how many retries are allowed. For example, a footnote in Anthropic's Opus 4.6 announcement says a prompt tweak takes SWE-bench Verified to 81.42%. When third parties rerun with uniform settings, scores usually come out a few points lower.

2 · Leaked past papers

The questions may already be memorized

Public exams get crawled into web data and then used to train the next generation of models. In February 2026 OpenAI said it would stop reporting SWE-bench Verified because scores increasingly reflect how many of the questions a model saw in training; it audited a set of hard problems and found most had flawed tests or problem statements.

3 · Maxed out

Past 95%, a point or two means nothing

The top Verified score is already 95%, and many remaining problems are themselves flawed. When third party vals.ai reran it, several models sat around 95%, and in September 2026 it stopped running the exam on new models altogether. At this point a 1–2 point gap is often just noise, which is why the industry is moving to harder exams like SWE-bench Pro and Terminal-Bench.

4 · Different exams

Each lab picks tests that favor it

In fall 2026, Anthropic's launch table led with Terminal-Bench and GDPval; OpenAI's GPT-6 led with DeepSWE; Google has its own mix. Different exams are hard to compare directly, and competitor scores in these tables are often copied from the other lab's announcement.

5 · Scores aren't experience

Testing well doesn't mean working well

Price, speed, whether it changes things you didn't ask for, how wordy it is: no exam measures these. Many engineers end up judging by how it feels on their own projects.

So how should you read them?

Three habits

Look at trends, not single scores; prefer third-party leaderboards that rerun every model with the same settings (such as Scale's SWE-bench Pro, vals.ai, or LMArena's user votes); then try it on your own work for a week.

02 · The capability curve

Coding ability went from 50% to 90% in two years

SWE-bench Verified is a 500-question exam. Each question is a GitHub issue from a real open-source project; the AI has to change the code itself, and the project's own tests decide whether it's fixed. At the end of 2024 the best publicly available model fixed about half (OpenAI announced 71.7% for o3 in December 2024, but it only shipped the following April); by mid-2026 the top score reached 95%.

What to look at

How fast the lines climb

At the end of 2024 the best publicly available model fixed about half the problems; by 2026 every lab's flagship was above 80%. This is exactly when agent tools took off, and the two fed each other.

What to look at

How far behind China's line is

Chinese models started far behind, but from 2025 the gap on this exam closed quickly, and most of them are open-weight. In April 2026 the DeepSeek V4 Pro preview self-reported 80.6%, while Claude Opus 4.7, out the same month, reported 87.6%. This exam is nearly maxed out, though. On the harder SWE-bench Pro or the third-party composite index, the gap is still large (see the table below). How the gap closed is covered on the Catching up and distillation page.

Note

These are vendor-reported scores

Each lab tests with different settings (thinking budget, agent harness, retries), so points can't be compared precisely. Just read the trend. Third-party reruns usually come out a few points lower, and this exam is nearly maxed out, so newer comparisons use Pro.

04 · Who is good at what

If you can only pick one, it depends on the job

Based on the third-party numbers above, the vendors' own tables, and reviews from several evaluation sites (November 2026). This is general direction, not a verdict.

Coding and long-running agent work

Claude Opus 5.5

Several review sites rate it among the best coding models publicly available; on the third-party composite index it trails only Fable 5.2, released in November, and Gemini 4 Carbon, which isn't public yet, at half Fable 5.2's price. To save money, try Sonnet 5.5 first: it scores even higher on terminal tasks at half the price.

The hardest reasoning, math and science

GPT-6 Astra

Third parties measured 95% on ARC-AGI-2 (abstract reasoning puzzles), and it leads on OpenAI's self-reported hard math tests; several sites call it the first choice for "the hardest reasoning problems". Priced a little above Fable 5.2. For everyday coding, GPT-6.1 Sol costs a fifth as much.

Long documents, video, general preference

Gemini 4 Argon

Ranked first among public models in LMArena user votes, meaning ordinary people most often prefer its answers. Long documents, long videos, and financial or legal documents are its recommended strengths. The promotional price ($2 input / $10 output per million tokens) only runs until 11/30. From 12/1 it is $4 / $20, the same as Opus 5.5.

The hardest tasks, writing

Claude Fable 5.2

Released 11/9, it is now first on the third-party composite index (64.6) and ties Opus 5.5 on terminal tasks. The previous version, Fable 5.1, was rated the best writer by several sites. Its price dropped to $8 / $40 per million tokens, still twice Opus 5.5, so you usually don't need it for everyday coding.

High-volume, low-cost everyday tasks

GPT-6 Luna, Claude Haiku 5.5, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Gemini 3.8 Flash

GPT-6 Luna, Claude Haiku 5.5 (both $0.10 / $0.50 per million tokens) and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash cost roughly a tenth to a hundredth of a flagship. Gemini 3.8 Flash costs more: about a fifth of Opus 5.5, or a third of Gemini 4 Argon's promo price. All are suited to classification, summarization and translation: lots of volume, not much difficulty. Cursor's Auto mode routes simple requests to this tier.

Self-hosting, open weights

Kimi K3.1 Pro, GLM-5.3, DeepSeek V4

You can download the weights and run them on your own servers. Kimi K3.1 Pro has the highest terminal-task score (67.2) in the whole comparison table. Watch out for data storage and censorship issues; see the Catching up and distillation page.

In plain wordsIt's like choosing a doctor: no one is best at everything, so first decide which specialty you need. For home use, the free versions of Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini are close; pick whichever feels comfortable.

There are two models most people can't use. Google's Gemini 4 Carbon (released 10/28) is so far available only to selected cyber defenders; Google says it will open it up as soon as possible. Anthropic's Mythos is not a separate, stronger model than Fable: according to Anthropic, they are the same underlying model, and Fable adds an extra layer of safeguards for biology, cybersecurity and requests that look like frontier AI development. Mythos, without that layer, is only for security partners.
