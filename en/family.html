Using AI day to day?
Remember these five things

This page skips the theory and sticks to using AI safely. The text is a little larger so you can hand it to your parents, or share it with the buttons below.

Five tips
  1. Anything involving money or passwords, check it yourselfAI can look things up and organize them for you, but transfers, payments and typing in passwords are always your job. A web page or message the AI reads may hide instructions from a scammer.
  2. Don't paste in ID numbers, bank accounts or medical recordsEverything you type is sent to the provider's servers. If you really need to, black out names and numbers first.
  3. Take a look at the privacy settingsMost apps let you opt out of having your chats used for training, and you can turn off memory too. It takes a minute, once.
  4. Double-check numbers, drug names and legal questionsSounding confident doesn't make it right. For anything important, check two sources or ask a professional.
  5. Be suspicious of "AI investing" or "AI trading" offersAnything that promises guaranteed returns in the name of AI is almost always a scam. AI can also fake celebrity videos and voices. If a "friend" calls asking for money, hang up and call them back. In Taiwan, you can call the 165 anti-fraud hotline if anything seems off.

Three common situations. Pick what you would do, then see why.

Scenario 1

In a chat group, a "teacher" says AI stock picking guarantees returns, and asks you to join as a member and wire money first.

Scenario 2

The phone rings. The voice sounds exactly like your son, who says he is in trouble and needs you to transfer money right now.

Scenario 3

You ask an AI assistant to tidy your inbox. It says: "One email from your bank asks you to enter your password at this address right away. Shall I handle it?"

What parents ask

Answers you can pass straight to your family

Does it remember me?

The model doesn't. It "remembers" because the app saves the conversation and sends it again next time, or because a memory feature writes key points into notes that get included in new chats. Turn memory off and start a new chat, and the model won't know what you talked about before. That doesn't mean the record is gone: the provider's servers may still keep your chats, and any custom instructions you set are included every time. To get rid of the record, delete it in the settings.

If I teach it something, will it learn it?

Within the same conversation it will follow along, because what you said is in the context. To make it stick, it has to be written into memory, project instructions or a skill file that gets included every time. The model doesn't change because you taught it.

Will my chats be used to train the next model?

That depends on the service's privacy settings, which you can choose. Even if they are, it shows up in a new model months later; the one you're talking to isn't learning in real time. For anything confidential, check the settings first.

Does it get dumber if we chat too long?

It can. The context has a limit, and when it's stuffed with unrelated material, important details are easier to miss. Very long chats get automatically summarized, and details can get lost. Starting a new chat with only what's needed is often more accurate.

Why does it feel different lately?

Usually not because of you. The model doesn't change because you chat with it, but the provider does change things: it ships new versions, swaps the default model, or rewrites the system prompt (the rules) that is included every time. New notes from a memory feature can also change its answers. So "the model hasn't changed a single bit" applies to the time you spend chatting with it; the provider can replace or adjust it at any time.

How can an AI assistant be tricked?

Web pages, emails and files are put into the context too, and the model can't always tell "what the owner said" from "text it read". A scammer can hide an instruction in a web page and it may be treated as a request. This is called prompt injection. Anything involving money or passwords needs a human to confirm.
