PART 4 · Catching up and distillation

How Chinese AI caught up,
and how US labs push back

At the end of 2024, China's best models still trailed US flagships by a wide margin on coding exams. By 2026 the gap on that nearly maxed-out exam had shrunk to a few points, and most of them are open-weight and much cheaper. On harder exams the gap is still clear: on SWE-bench Pro, Qwen3.8 Max scores 67.7% to Opus 5.5's 89.9%, and on the third-party composite index the best Chinese model scores 46.5 to the best US model, Fable 5.2, at 64.6 (see Benchmarks). Engineering innovation is one of the main reasons; "distilling" other companies' models is the most controversial one.

Three things on this page
  1. Chinese models closed much of the gap in two years, mostly with open weights and low prices, but on harder exams the gap is still clear.
  2. There are four reasons they moved so fast, and only one is disputed: distillation, which means training your own model on another lab's answers.
  3. The accusations are the accusers' account. Nobody has published how much the industry blocks, and Chinese releases show no sign of slowing so far.

01 · The rise

A few key players

When DeepSeek R1 launched in January 2025, many people realized for the first time how close Chinese models had come. After that, several companies took turns releasing strong open-weight models. For how the scores changed, see the capability curve on the benchmarks page.

DeepSeekV3 (2024.12), R1 (2025.01)V3 is a 671B-parameter MoE model that activates only 37B per token; its technical report says full training took 2.788 million H800 GPU hours, and notes that this covers only the final training run, not the earlier research and experiments. R1-Zero, released alongside it, learned step-by-step reasoning from reinforcement learning alone; R1 itself first went through a round of supervised fine-tuning, then reinforcement learning. The paper was later published in Nature.

02 · Why they caught up so fast

Four reasons, only one of them controversial

Reason 1 · Engineering innovation

Squeezing every bit of efficiency out of limited compute

MoE (activating only a small part of the parameters each time), MLA (compressing the memory attention needs), low-precision training, and training reasoning with reinforcement learning. Labs around the world later borrowed many of these techniques.

Reason 2 · Export controls backfire

Can't buy the best chips, so be more frugal

The US restricts exports of advanced AI chips to China, which pushed Chinese teams to work harder on algorithms and systems. That's one reason their efficiency stands out.

Reason 3 · Open weights and low prices

Anyone in the world can download and modify them

Open weights let developers worldwide use them and report problems together, so the ecosystem moves fast; API prices are often a fraction of US flagships'.

Reason 4 · Controversial

Distilling other companies' models

In early 2026, Anthropic and OpenAI both publicly accused some Chinese labs of using large numbers of accounts to query their models and training their own models on the answers. Details below.

In plain wordsA student whose grades improve fast might be a great, efficient learner, or might be secretly copying the top student's notes. In reality it's often both, and looking at only one side isn't fair.

03 · What distillation is

A student model learns by imitating a teacher model's answers

Distillation itself is an ordinary technique: use a large model's (the teacher's) outputs as teaching material to train a smaller, cheaper model (the student). Every lab uses it to shrink its own big models, and DeepSeek also distilled R1's reasoning into smaller models. The problem is when "the teacher belongs to someone else, who explicitly forbids it".

STEP 1Prepare questionsGenerate large numbers of prompts targeting the skills you want (coding, tool use, reasoning).
STEP 2Ask the teacher modelSend them through the API, often asking it to "write out the reasoning step by step".
STEP 3Collect the answersTurn the questions, answers and reasoning into training data.
STEP 4Train the studentSupervised fine-tuning on this data, or use the teacher as a grader for reinforcement learning.
RESULTThe student picks up the teacher's "problem-solving style"Similar abilities, without spending what the teacher spent on data and training.
What distillation training data looks like (illustrative)jsonl
{"prompt": "Fix this race condition…",
 "teacher_reasoning": "First look at the lock scope… step two…",  // the most valuable part
 "teacher_answer": "Move the mutex to…"}
{"prompt": "Use these three tools to find…",
 "teacher_tool_calls": [ … ],
 "teacher_answer": "…"}

Why is the reasoning the most valuable part? With only answers, the student can only memorize; with the full thinking steps, it learns "how to think". So one focus of anti-distillation is keeping the full reasoning from being taken.

In plain wordsDistillation is like asking the top student to write worked solutions for a big stack of problems and using them to train your own students. That's normal at the top student's own school; a rival cram school sending in fake students to collect the solutions in bulk is another matter.

04 · Public accusations

From suspicion to naming names

Every accusation below is the accuser's own account. Most of the companies named have not responded publicly, and Alibaba declined to comment on the June accusation. China's government, however, publicly rejected the claims twice, in July and September 2026; those responses are listed too.

  1. 2025.01After DeepSeek R1 launchedOpenAI and Microsoft reportedly investigated whether someone had distilled OpenAI's models through the API.
  2. 2025.09Anthropic tightens sales restrictionsIt stops serving companies controlled from China and other regions (including subsidiaries more than 50% owned), citing among other reasons that they might use distillation to build their own AI.
  3. 2026.02.12OpenAI's memo to the US House Select Committee on ChinaSays people linked to DeepSeek developed ways around access restrictions, getting US model outputs through "third-party routers that mask the source".
  4. 2026.02GoogleSays a single campaign sent Gemini more than 100,000 prompts trying to replicate the model, calling it intellectual property theft.
  5. 2026.02.23Anthropic's report names DeepSeek, Moonshot and MiniMaxAbout 24,000 fraudulent accounts and over 16 million conversations. By Anthropic's count: MiniMax over 13 million, Moonshot over 3.4 million, DeepSeek over 150,000. Targets were agentic coding, tool use and reasoning, and DeepSeek is also said to have used Claude as a grader for reinforcement learning.
  6. 2026.04.23White House OSTP memo NSTM-4"Adversarial Distillation of American AI Models", signed by OSTP Director Michael Kratsios and sent to the heads of all federal agencies. It says foreign entities, principally based in China, are engaged in "deliberate, industrial-scale campaigns" to distill US frontier AI, using tens of thousands of proxy accounts and jailbreaking. The memo names no specific companies. Its listed responses are sharing information with US AI companies, helping industry coordinate defenses, developing best practices together, and exploring ways to hold foreign actors accountable.
  7. 2026.06Anthropic accuses AlibabaIn a June 10 letter to US senators and White House officials (reported on 6/24), Anthropic said operators linked to Alibaba's Qwen lab used nearly 25,000 fraudulent accounts for about 28.8 million exchanges with Claude between 4/22 and 6/5, the largest distillation attack it had seen. Alibaba declined to comment.
  8. 2026.07.27China's Commerce Ministry responds for the first timeAfter US officials accused Moonshot AI of distilling US models to build Kimi K3 and threatened investigations and sanctions, a ministry spokesperson said the accusations lacked factual evidence and legal basis.
  9. 2026.09.08Joint NSA, CISA and FBI advisoryNames six labs (DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Zhipu) and says they have taken billions of tokens from US models since 2024. It is the first time US intelligence and cybersecurity agencies jointly named six labs; before this, a White House memo and officials' statements had already made the accusation publicly.
  10. 2026.09.09China's Commerce Ministry responds to the advisoryAnswering reporters the next day: the US claims have no factual or legal basis; distillation is essentially a neutral technique used by model companies worldwide, including American ones; and if the US uses anti-distillation as a pretext to suppress Chinese companies, China will take firm countermeasures.
  11. 2026.10.12Anthropic's second reportSays distillers later scaled up, building proxy networks that control hundreds of thousands of fraudulent accounts; new defenses cut the data they obtained by about 90–99%. It also adds Zhipu for distilling security-related capabilities.
  12. 2026.10US Homeland Security orders DeepSeek removed from federal systemsPublished in October, the order requires federal civilian agencies to remove DeepSeek products from their IT systems, including third-party products built with DeepSeek, and bars new purchases. It is a security and procurement order, not about distillation, but it reflects the same wariness toward Chinese AI.
05 · Attack and defense in detail

How the attackers copy, how the defenders block

The techniques described in the public reports, paired up. Each attack is followed by the matching defense.

Attacker: the distillerDefender: the model company
ATTACK · ScaleMasses of fake accounts + proxy networksTraffic spread over many accounts, backed by what Anthropic calls "hydra cluster" proxy networks. In February a single network managed over 20,000 accounts at once; by October, Anthropic said these proxy networks controlled hundreds of thousands of fraudulent accounts. Ban one and another takes its place.
vs
DEFENSE · DetectionBehavioral fingerprints and classifiersLinking accounts into a single campaign through IP correlation, request metadata and infrastructure traits; a two-stage classifier recognizes distillation patterns, with a false positive rate Anthropic puts below 0.1%.
ATTACK · Hiding the sourceThird-party routers and resellersInstead of using their own accounts directly, they go through third-party services that resell API access, so requests appear to come from someone else.
vs
DEFENSE · IdentityAccount verification and regional limitsStronger verification for account types that are easy to impersonate, such as education, security research and startups; no service to China-controlled companies; sharing intelligence with cloud providers and other labs.
ATTACK · Extracting reasoningAsking the model to write out its full thinkingPrompts designed to make the model spell out its reasoning step by step, or to reconstruct reasoning traces afterwards, because that's the most instructive part.
vs
DEFENSE · Reasoning stays inShow only summaries, limit where reasoning goesShow the reasoning only as a summary, or only to the organization that made the request. Anthropic's experiment: it trained a pretrained Claude model on Claude Opus 4.8 transcripts. Trained on full reasoning it scored 80.4% on SWE-bench Verified; trained on summaries only, it dropped to 71.8%.
ATTACK · Targeted skillsCopy only the most expensive skillsHeavily concentrated on agentic coding, tool use, computer use and grading: the capabilities whose data is hardest to produce yourself.
vs
DEFENSE · CostBans, rate limits, and charging anywayTighter limits on repeat offenders, outright bans, and charging even for blocked requests, to raise the cost of copying.
ATTACK · Research frontierAny visible output can be teaching materialAs long as the API returns text, it can in theory be used for training. That's the root reason distillation is so hard to stop completely.
vs
DEFENSE · Research frontierAntidistillation samplingA 2025 paper proposes slightly adjusting how the model picks the next token, so its reasoning traces train other models less effectively, with little effect on quality for ordinary users. Still at the research stage.

06 · How much is blocked

How well do the defenses work, and have Chinese releases slowed?

The short answer: only Anthropic has published a number, saying distillers now get over 90% less data from its own platform. Nobody has published how much is blocked across the industry. As of November 2026, Chinese releases show no clear slowdown because of it.

Vendor-reported · Anthropic, Oct 202690–99%

Less reasoning data taken from Claude

Once the new defenses were fully in place, the reasoning distillers could extract from Claude fell 10 to 100 times, on some days too little to measure. Tens of thousands of accounts were banned, many within two hours of starting. The classifier's false-positive rate is under 0.1%. This is Anthropic's own estimate, for its own platform only, with no third-party audit.

US government · Sep 8, 20266 labs

NSA, CISA and FBI name names

A joint advisory (AA26-251A) names DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Zhipu (Z.ai). It says they have taken "billions of tokens" from US models since 2024, and that distillation is core to their development strategy. It recommends defenses but gives no blocking rate.

Academia · Oxford et al., Sep 2026Gap closed

Summaries alone won't hold for long

Researchers used a small model to expand reasoning summaries back into near-complete traces, then trained on them. After reinforcement learning, the student nearly matched one trained on full traces. The experiments used models of 3B parameters or fewer and math problems only, so they don't directly carry over to the largest models.

Have Chinese releases slowed?

Not visibly. Chinese labs kept shipping in the second half of 2026: Moonshot's Kimi K3 (July), Zhipu's GLM-5.3 (August), an updated DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (October) and Kimi K3.1 Pro (October).

There were delays, but not because of distillation. DeepSeek V4 slipped twice, which analysts blamed on the heavy re-engineering needed to move to Huawei chips. Alibaba showed Qwen 4 at its September conference but had given no release date by late October, without saying why.

The defenses make distillation harder, but haven't stopped it. Anthropic says that while building GLM-5.3, Zhipu tried to distill Claude's cyber capabilities. When it found the newest model's safeguards weakened its attacks, it switched to an older Claude model and another lab's model. GLM-5.3 shipped anyway.

The gap is roughly 4 to 7 months. By the AI 2027 Tracker's count, the best Chinese model trails the best US model by about 4 to 7 months, depending on the test. The gap is larger on newer kinds of tests.

A balanced reading: the new defenses were only fully in place by September and October 2026, which is too soon to show up in models that were already trained. The real test will be China's next generation of models in 2027. The 90–99% figure also covers Anthropic alone, not other labs' traffic, and Anthropic itself says: "We expect that attackers will find ways around these safeguards."

In plain wordsThe famous teacher now writes only key points on the board, and the note-copiers get over 90% less. But these students were already capable, they can copy from another teacher's class, or practice on their own to fill in the gaps. So for now, there's no sign they're failing to hand in their homework.

07 · Where the controversy lies

It's not as black and white as it looks

Is it legal?

Breaking terms of service isn't breaking the law

Every lab forbids using outputs to train competing models, and fake accounts violate the terms too. But whether model outputs can be copyrighted is unsettled in every country, so it's mostly a business and policy question.

Double standard?

US models used other people's data too

Critics point out that large models were trained on huge amounts of other people's content from the web. Supporters argue that systematically taking a competitor's work through fake accounts is different in kind.

China's government

"A neutral technique; the claims are groundless"

China's Commerce Ministry responded twice, in July and September 2026: the US accusations lack factual and legal basis; distillation is a neutral practice used worldwide, including by US companies; and China will retaliate if the US uses it as a pretext against Chinese firms. Most of the companies named have not responded themselves.

Security and policy

Distillation can also get around export controls

Anthropic argues distillation undermines chip export controls, and that distilled models don't necessarily keep the original's safety protections. That turns a business dispute into a geopolitical issue.

A balanced conclusion: Chinese labs do have a lot of real skill, as their published papers and open-weight models show; at the same time, the alleged distillation was large-scale, and US labs are pushing back with technology and policy. Both can be true.

In plain wordsAnti-distillation is like a famous teacher who writes only key points on the board instead of handing out full solutions, and who recognizes the people auditing the class under a hundred fake names. It stops most of them, but not all.

08 · What family will ask

Can I use Chinese AI apps?

Yes, but know three things

Where your data is stored. With a Chinese company's own app or website, conversations are usually stored on servers in China, subject to Chinese law, and may legally have to be handed over in government investigations. Taiwan's government agencies have banned official use of DeepSeek since 2025, and in October 2026 the US Department of Homeland Security published an order for federal civilian agencies to remove DeepSeek products.

Content is censored. On politically sensitive topics (such as Taiwan or Tiananmen), answers often dodge the question or follow the official line. When researching or learning, don't rely on it alone for these topics.

The app and the model are different things. Many Chinese models are open-weight, so other companies can host them on servers in other countries. Then your data doesn't go through the original company, but the censorship tendencies trained into the model remain.

So what should I do?

Don't share personal data, work documents, or account passwords; check several sources on political and historical topics; and watch the permissions a mobile app asks for when you install it. These apply to any AI app, really; just take extra care with Chinese apps.
