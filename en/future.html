PART 5 · The next bottleneck

The next bottleneck:
power, memory and storage

Models keep getting stronger, but letting more people use them for longer is running into real-world limits: the power grid, data centers, memory chips, and storage. This page looks at those four, and how they connect to the context and video models discussed earlier.

Three things on this page
  1. Data center electricity use is projected to double by 2030, and five big companies are spending more than $600 billion a year.
  2. AI memory chips (HBM) are sold out, and memory prices for phones and computers are rising with them.
  3. High-quality public text is running out, so the next models lean on video and audio, and hard drives are in short supply too.

  • Data center electricity grew 17% in 2025Electricity use at AI-focused data centers grew even faster, by 50%.
  • Racks are getting "hotter"The IEA notes the power density of AI servers rose about 11× from 2020 to 2025. Fans can't keep up, so liquid cooling has become standard.
  • But energy per task is falling fastThe IEA's 2026 update says the energy to complete the same AI task drops by at least an order of magnitude each year. Total use keeps rising because users and tasks grow even faster.
  • Tech companies are sourcing their own powerMicrosoft signed with Constellation to restart Three Mile Island Unit 1 (835 MW); Google with Kairos and Amazon with X-energy invest in small modular reactors (SMRs); Meta announced nuclear deals of up to 6.6 GW.

02 · Data centers and investment

A building boom of over $600 billion a year

Estimates from early 2026 put the combined capital spending of Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta and Oracle above $600 billion, with roughly three quarters going to AI infrastructure.

Scale

From MW to GW

Traditional data centers are measured in tens of MW; AI campuses are starting to be measured in GW. The Stargate project announced in January 2025 targets investment of up to $500 billion.

The real bottlenecks

Transformers, grid connections, cooling

The chips can be bought, but grid connections have years-long queues, and large transformers and turbine generators have long lead times. Liquid cooling, energy storage and on-site power plants have become site-selection criteria.

Link to the tech page

Inference will use more than training

Training happens once; inference runs for every user and every round of conversation. One agent task can make dozens of requests, each resending the whole context, so total demand scales with usage.

Back to Taiwan

Chips

Almost all AI chips are made in Taiwan

The most advanced process nodes and CoWoS advanced packaging for Nvidia, AMD and Google AI chips are mainly at TSMC. A large share of the orders from this building boom ends up in Taiwan.

Power

Taiwan is worried about power too

In 2024 the Ministry of Economic Affairs said it would no longer approve power for large data centers over 5 MW north of Taoyuan, hoping new data centers would move to central and southern Taiwan. Semiconductor expansion plus AI has made power supply a long-running issue.

Memory

Price rises matter to Taiwanese makers too

Prices at Taiwanese memory makers such as Nanya Technology and Winbond follow this wave of DRAM price increases; the prices you see when buying phones, computers and memory cards are part of the same story.

03 · Memory

Longer contexts and more users mean not enough memory

No matter how fast the GPU computes, the model weights and every conversation's KV cache have to sit next to it in high-bandwidth memory (HBM). The prompt cache from the How it works page is exactly this.

  • HBM eats production capacityA Micron executive says each GB of HBM uses about three times the wafer capacity of regular DRAM. The more HBM is made, the less DRAM is left for phones and computers.
  • 2026 capacity sold out long agoAt its October 2025 earnings call, SK hynix said its 2026 HBM, DRAM and NAND capacity was "essentially sold out".
  • Ordinary people feel it tooA Samsung executive said in January 2026 the shortage "will affect everyone"; reports say a 32GB DDR5 stick rose from $149 in September 2025 to $239. Computers and phones getting pricier or cutting specs is related.
How KV cache size is calculatedformula

per token = 2 (K,V) × layers × KV heads × head dim × bytes per value

Taking the openly documented Llama 3.1 70B as an example (80 layers, 8 KV heads, dimension 128, BF16 at 2 bytes per value), each token takes about 0.33 MB (327,680 bytes, counted in decimal units to match the GB in the calculator below). This model actually supports up to 128K; taking the calculator to 1M only illustrates how much memory a longer context would need.

In plain wordsBefore AI answers you, it has to spread your whole notebook out on the desk. The thicker the notebook and the more people reading notebooks at once, the bigger the desk has to be. The whole world is scrambling for these "desks" now, which is why memory in phones and computers got more expensive too.

04 · Storage and the next generation of models

Text is running out; next comes video and audio

There's only so much good public text on the internet; the research group Epoch AI estimates it could be used up in the second half of the 2020s. Next-generation models rely more and more on video, audio, synthetic data and reinforcement learning environments, and video and audio data are orders of magnitude larger than text.

Video in the context

One hour of video ≈ a million tokens

By Gemini's published accounting, an hour of video is about 1 million tokens of context. Whether reading or generating it, video models use more memory and compute than text.

Training data

From TB to EB

Text-only training data is on the order of tens of TB; video datasets easily reach PB or EB. The data has to be stored, cleaned and read repeatedly, which takes both hard drives and SSDs.

During training

A single checkpoint is several TB

Training saves checkpoints regularly in case of crashes. Rough estimate: a 1-trillion-parameter model with optimizer state takes about 14 bytes per parameter, so about 14 TB per save, and one training run saves many times.

  • Hard drives sold outWestern Digital's CEO said in early 2026 that its 2026 capacity was "pretty much sold out", with some enterprise contracts running to 2028. Reports say enterprise hard drive lead times stretched to 12 months.
  • A big shift to QLC flashIndustry insiders say cloud providers buy tens to hundreds of EB of flash storage a year, and high-capacity QLC SSDs are starting to replace some hard drives. SSD prices are rising as a result.
  • The direction of model training is changingNatively multimodal models (hearing, seeing and speaking at once), "world models" that understand the physical world, and reinforcement learning practice in simulated environments. What they share: bigger data, more compute and more storage.

In plain wordsAI used to read books; now it's starting to watch movies and listen to radio. One movie file is worth tens of thousands of books. So the world's hard drives are being bought up to hold these videos.

05 · Common questions

How does this affect ordinary people?

Will electricity bills go up because of AI?

It depends on local grids and regulations. In areas where data centers cluster, there's already debate over who should pay for new power plants and transmission lines. The IEA also notes that data centers tend to be concentrated in a few places, so the pressure on local grids is greater than global figures suggest.

Why do phones and computers seem more expensive?

Memory factories give priority to more profitable HBM and server memory, so consumer products get less, and prices rise. Most of the industry expects this shortage to last at least into 2027.

Will AI always use this much power?

Energy per task is falling fast, and models keep getting more efficient (prompt cache, small models and distillation, as discussed earlier). But users and tasks grow even faster, so total electricity use will keep rising in the short term. Nobody can say for sure what happens in the long run.
