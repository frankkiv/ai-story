AI grew hands.
From chat to agents.

AI went from a consultant that talks to a team of assistants that do the work, and you can direct them from your phone. But it isn't actually "learning" you. The model itself never changes; what changes is the set of "notes" handed to it every time, which engineers call the context. Five parts explain how it works, with practical tips for your family at the end.

For family · The practical part

So what should you watch out for when using AI?

The rest of the site covers how things work. In daily life it comes down to these five things. Feel free to forward this section to your family.

  1. Anything involving money or passwords, check it yourselfAI can look things up and organize them for you, but transfers, payments and typing in passwords are always your job. A web page or message the AI reads may hide instructions from a scammer.
  2. Don't paste in ID numbers, bank accounts or medical recordsEverything you type is sent to the provider's servers. If you really need to, black out names and numbers first.
  3. Take a look at the privacy settingsMost apps let you opt out of having your chats used for training, and you can turn off memory too. It takes a minute, once.
  4. Double-check numbers, drug names and legal questionsSounding confident doesn't make it right. For anything important, check two sources or ask a professional.
  5. Be suspicious of "AI investing" or "AI trading" offersAnything that promises guaranteed returns in the name of AI is almost always a scam. AI can also fake celebrity videos and voices. If a "friend" calls asking for money, hang up and call them back.
What parents ask

Answers you can pass straight to your family

Does it remember me?

The model doesn't. It "remembers" because the app saves the conversation and sends it again next time, or because a memory feature writes key points into notes that get included in new chats. Turn memory off and start a new chat, and it's meeting you for the first time.

If I teach it something, will it learn it?

Within the same conversation it will follow along, because what you said is in the context. To make it stick, it has to be written into memory, project instructions or a skill file that gets included every time. The model doesn't change because you taught it.

Will my chats be used to train the next model?

That depends on the service's privacy settings, which you can choose. Even if they are, it shows up in a new model months later; the one you're talking to isn't learning in real time. For anything confidential, check the settings first.

Does it get dumber if we chat too long?

It can. The context has a limit, and when it's stuffed with unrelated material, important details are easier to miss. Very long chats get automatically summarized, and details can get lost. Starting a new chat with only what's needed is often more accurate.

How can an AI assistant be tricked?

Web pages, emails and files are put into the context too, and the model can't always tell "what the owner said" from "text it read". A scammer can hide an instruction in a web page and it may be treated as a request. This is called prompt injection. Anything involving money or passwords needs a human to confirm.
